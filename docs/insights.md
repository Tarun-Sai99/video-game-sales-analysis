# Key insights

A summary of what the analysis found, one answer per business question. All figures are VGChartz sales in millions of units for games released up to 2018 (6,592.57M units in total). Details and full tables are in [analysis_notes.md](analysis_notes.md).

## 1. How did the market grow and decline over the years?

- Tracked sales grew slowly through the 1980s and 1990s, then jumped in the 2000s. **The 2000s were the biggest decade with 3,324.95M units**, about half of everything tracked.
- **Sales peaked in 2008 at 538.09M units**, the Wii, Xbox 360, PS3 and DS generation. 2007 to 2011 were the five strongest years.
- After 2011, sales fell to 148.03M in 2018. Part of this is real (the move to digital downloads and mobile games) and part is VGChartz tracking fewer games.

## 2. Which genres sell best, and how has the mix changed?

- **Sports, Action and Shooter make up half of all tracked sales** (3,306.56M units together).
- Sports led the 2000s (619.52M). **Shooters became the top genre of the 2010s** (560.41M), up from 4th place.
- Platform games peaked early and shrank later, while Role-Playing grew from the 2000s to the 2010s when most genres fell.

## 3. Which platforms dominated each era?

- Each era had a clear leader: **Atari in the 1970s and 1980s, then Sony from the 1990s onwards** (61% of 1990s sales, about 49% in the 2000s and 2010s).
- **Sony accounts for 49% of all tracked sales** (3,258.99M units), almost twice Nintendo's total.
- **Microsoft grew from 17% to 31%** between the 2000s and the 2010s and passed Nintendo, whose share fell from 31% to 17%.
- Sega fell from 9% of 1990s sales to almost nothing after it stopped making consoles.

## 4. Who are the top publishers and best-selling games?

- **Activision is the top publisher** (722.77M units), mainly thanks to Call of Duty, which holds **7 of the 10 best-selling game spots**.
- **Grand Theft Auto V is the best-selling game** (64.29M units across platforms), more than twice the next game.
- **Rockstar Games sells the most per game:** 239.67M units from only 93 games (about 2.6M per game), while Activision needed 1,044 games.
- The top 10 publishers sold 57% of all tracked units.

## 5. How do the regions differ?

- **North America buys half of all tracked games** (51%), Europe 29%, Japan 10% and the rest of the world 10%.
- North America and Europe buy almost the same mix: Sports, Action and Shooter are the top 3 in both.
- **Japan is different.** Role-Playing is its #1 genre (18.63% of its sales, against about 5% elsewhere). Fighting, Strategy and Puzzle games also do better there, while Shooters (5.00%) and Racing (2.90%) barely sell.

## Recommendations

These are written as if for a game publisher planning new releases.

1. **Plan around North America and Europe for action, sports and shooter games.** Together they are 80% of sales and share the same taste.
2. **Treat Japan as its own market.** An RPG or fighting game should include Japan in its core plan. A Western shooter should not expect much from it.
3. **Build franchises.** Call of Duty and Grand Theft Auto show that a few strong series can outsell large catalogues of single games.
4. **Release on the leading consoles.** Sony and Microsoft took almost 80% of 2010s sales.

## Limitations of the data

- **Sales stop around 2018.** VGChartz tracked very few games after 2018 (only 63 games from 2019 onwards have sales), so the trend analysis ends at 2018.
- **Only boxed retail sales are counted.** Digital downloads, app stores and subscriptions are missing, so PC and mobile games look much smaller than they are.
- **Some big games have no sales figures.** Most of Nintendo's own hits, such as Mario Kart Wii and Mario Kart 8 Deluxe, have blank sales, so Nintendo's real share is higher than shown.
- **Publisher names are not merged.** "Electronic Arts" and "EA Sports" are listed separately. Together they would be the top publisher (1,129.79M units).
- **Units, not revenue.** The data counts copies sold, not money earned, so a cheap game and an expensive game count the same.
